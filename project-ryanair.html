Ryanair: from reputation crisis to digital travel company

Corporate Financial Management, University of Basel · group project, team of three

  • Financial analysis
  • Business model
  • Strategy
  • Benchmarking
Question
Why did Europe's lowest-cost airline have to change its business model in 2013, and did the change pay off?
What I did
Covered the second half of the case: why change was a need and not just an opportunity (flat demand, a reputation crisis, rivals pairing low fares with better service), the two-phase pivot from 2013 to 2018, its financial results, today's position and recommendations to 2030.
Outcome
Showed that growth came from profit efficiency and not fare increases: revenue rose from about €5bn to €7bn, operating margin from 10% to 20%, and unit cost per passenger fell from about €55 to €42. Closed with six recommendations for 2025–2030.

Highlights

  • Phase 1 (2013–2015): customer experience, with allocated seating, lower fees and a new website and app.
  • Phase 2 (2015–2018): platform expansion into hotels, car rental and transfers, backed by Ryanair Labs.
  • EBITDA margin climbed from 15% to 30%.
  • Ancillary revenues grew at about 25% a year.
  • Recommendations cover people, costs, digital, sustainability, revenue and brand.
