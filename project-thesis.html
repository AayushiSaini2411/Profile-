Beyond Compliance: sustainable supply chains under environmental policy pressure

Master's thesis, University of Basel · energy-intensive SMEs in Europe · submitted October 2026

  • R
  • Multilevel data
  • OLS regression
  • Interaction models
  • Wild cluster bootstrap
  • Policy analysis
Question
How do institutional and technological conditions influence sustainable supply chain implementation under environmental policy pressure in energy-intensive SMEs across Europe?
What I did
Combined firm-level survey data from Flash Eurobarometer 549 with country indicators for policy stringency (OECD), rule of law (World Bank), digital integration (DESI), GDP per capita and energy import dependence. Built an index of five resource-efficiency practices and tested three hypotheses in R. The data is multilevel, with firms nested in countries, which I handled with OLS regression, interaction terms and country-clustered standard errors, checked with a wild cluster bootstrap.
Outcome
Stricter policy goes with more implementation, but modestly: one standard deviation more stringency corresponds to about a third of an additional practice. Neither institutional quality nor digital integration strengthens that link. Firm size is the only predictor that holds in every specification.

Highlights

  • Sample: 311 energy-intensive SMEs across 21 EU member states, surveyed in June 2024. 55.3% employ fewer than 50 people.
  • Operational practices are already widespread: 77.8% of firms minimise waste and 74.3% save materials.
  • Process practices follow policy stringency and firm size; eco-design follows institutional quality and energy import dependence.
  • The results held under a different index construction, a different stringency measure, ordered logit, reduced country samples and bootstrap inference.
  • For policymakers: product-level change such as repairability may need different instruments from operational resource efficiency.
  • For support programmes: firm capability and programme reach look like the limiting factors, more than the strictness of national policy.
  • The evidence is associational, from a single cross-section, so these are considerations and not recommendations.

Tools I used

  • Excel
  • R
  • Canva
  • Python

What I learned

  • To combine data from several sources and two levels, firm and country, into one dataset.
  • To report a result that did not confirm my hypotheses honestly, and to test it from several angles.
  • To turn statistical results into considerations for policy and business, with their limits stated.
