Coins are cold, cards are caring: do gifts in donation letters work?

Seminar on Social Preferences and Fundraising, University of Basel · solo presentation

  • Behavioural economics
  • Experimental evidence
  • Mediation analysis
  • Critical review
  • Presenting
Question
Do small gifts enclosed with donation letters make people more generous, or do they backfire?
What I did
Presented and critically reviewed the paper by Yin, Li and Singh on my own: the theory of communal versus exchange norms, four hypotheses, seven lab and field studies and the mediation analysis, then drew out what it means for fundraisers.
Outcome
A coin gets the letter opened but lowers the gift. In the main field experiment the average donation was USD 17.48 with a coin and USD 47.50 with no gift, and sending no gift gave the best return in every study.

Highlights

  • Letters with a coin were opened and read by 48% of recipients, against 17–26% for a card or no gift.
  • Greeting cards did no better than sending nothing.
  • Mechanism: a coin makes the relationship feel like a transaction and not an act of care.
  • Among 2,643 existing donors, the return per letter was USD 2.94 with no gift, 1.53 with a magnet and 0.75 with a coin.
  • Recommendation: use coins only to reach new audiences; for existing donors a plain ask raises the most.
