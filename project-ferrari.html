Ferrari IPO: valuing a car maker as a luxury brand

Business Cases in Strategy, Finance and Entrepreneurship, University of Basel · team of four · November 2024

  • Valuation
  • DCF
  • EV/EBITDA multiples
  • WACC
  • SWOT
Question
Was the 2015 IPO the right decision for Ferrari, and what was a fair price per share?
What I did
Prepared two parts of the case myself. First, the IPO itself: how the process works step by step, its pros and cons for Ferrari, and the alternatives to going public. Second, the discounted cash flow valuation, with a 5.52% WACC. A teammate valued Ferrari with an EV/EBITDA multiple weighted 30% car manufacturer and 70% luxury goods (10.5x), which we set against my DCF.
Outcome
My DCF gave USD 57.42 per share and the multiples approach USD 43.20, either side of the actual IPO price of USD 52. Concluded that going public was the right decision, with the value driven by the mix of car and luxury positioning and a strong brand.

Highlights

  • Peer multiples: 7.7x EV/EBITDA for car manufacturers and 11.6x for luxury goods.
  • IPO on 21 October 2015: market capitalisation of about USD 10.4bn, and shares closed the first day at USD 55.
  • By market value, Ferrari ranked 4th in the car segment and 5th in the luxury segment.
  • Alternatives weighed: private equity, debt financing, joint ventures and staying fully under FCA.
  • SWOT analysis, with category and segment expansion recommended for the future.
