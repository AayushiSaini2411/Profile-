From Prestige to Purpose: sustainability reporting in EU luxury fashion

US Seminar 2026 · presented at Georgetown & NYU

  • ESG data
  • CSRD / ESRS
  • GRI
  • Benchmarking
  • R
Question
Have EU regulation and reputational pressure led to stronger environmental disclosure and performance among luxury fashion houses between 2020 and 2024?
What I did
Extracted water use intensity, waste recycling, renewable energy share and Scope 1+2 emissions from five years of ESG reports for 10 major luxury firms, benchmarked against ESRS indicators and cross-validated with MSCI, CDP and Sustainalytics.
Outcome
Mandatory EU rules gave more complete and consistent disclosure than voluntary reporting, and large conglomerates scored higher on environmental performance than independent maisons. Presented at Georgetown and NYU.

Highlights

  • Sample: 3 conglomerates (LVMH, Kering, Richemont) and 7 independent maisons, 2020–2024.
  • Four indicators: Scope 1+2 emissions, renewable energy share, waste recycling and water-use intensity.
  • Mean waste recycling rose from 60.6% in 2020 to 78.1% in 2023.
  • Several non-EU firms disclosed under 60% of the required indicators.
  • Company figures cross-checked against MSCI, CDP and Sustainalytics.
